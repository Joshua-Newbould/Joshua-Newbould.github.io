I am a PhD student at Durham University working on generative models, in particular flow matching, and on how to train them without hyperscale infrastructure.

My recent work, GridFlow, partitions the flow matching objective across both data and time, so a grid of independent blocks can be trained in isolation. This lowers per-device memory and inference cost, and at matched training compute the best grids outperform a single monolithic model on CIFAR-10. It was presented as an oral at the NeurIPS 2026 workshop on decentralised training of foundation models.

More broadly, I am interested in making large-scale generative modelling practical on smaller, distributed hardware, and in careful evaluation of what AI systems can do. I am a contributor to Humanity's Last Exam, published in Nature.

Research

Current interests

  • Flow matching and generative models

    Training and sampling generative models with flow matching, and how the objective can be split across data and time.

  • Decentralised, communication-free training

    Training large models as independent experts and time blocks, without synchronisation between devices.

  • Efficient large-scale generation

    Reducing per-device memory and inference cost so strong generative models can be built on distributed, high-latency hardware.

  • Evaluating AI capabilities

    Expert-level benchmarks that probe the frontier of what AI systems can and cannot do.
